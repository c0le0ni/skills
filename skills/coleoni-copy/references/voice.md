# The voice

The default voice for any product, site or interface, used when the project has
no voice guide of its own. When it has one, the project's guide wins and this
file fills the gaps.

The test for every sentence: **would the reader say it this way, about their own
situation?** If not, it is the writer talking to themselves.

---

## Part 1. What never changes

### 1. Short sentences. One idea each.

> Scope, deadline and price up front. No fine print.

If a sentence needs a semicolon, it is two sentences.

### 2. Things beat adjectives.

Name the real thing: the order, the invoice, the dashboard, the 48 hours. "A
complete, professional solution" says nothing a reader can check.

> A 200-line test becomes 20.

That line proves more than three sentences about efficiency.

### 3. No word the reader would not use.

If a technical idea matters, say what the reader gets from it. "Server-side
rendering" becomes "pages open fast, even on a weak connection".

### 4. Show, don't claim.

Out: *revolutionize, transform your business, next level, cutting-edge,
world-class, best-in-class, seamless, innovative, game-changer, unlock,
empower, elevate, supercharge, the leading, the best*.

A capability is shown (a number, a screenshot, a real example), never
announced. The reader draws the conclusion.

### 5. Don't assume what the reader wants.

"A site built to bring you customers" assumes everyone wants customers. Some
want credibility, a catalog, a portfolio, a tool for their team. Talk about
fit and possibility, not one goal you picked for them.

### 6. No proof that does not exist.

No invented numbers, cases, testimonials, client logos or counters. If the
project has no proof yet, the copy does without it. A good page is its own proof.

### 7. Form

- **No em dashes (—).** Readers now take them as the mark of machine-written
  text. Use a comma, a colon, parentheses or a full stop.
- **No decorative capitals.** A small uppercase label is fine when it labels
  something. Shouting a headline is not.
- **No emoji** in interfaces, product copy or documentation.
- **Exclamation marks: almost never.** Earned excitement does not need one.
- **Numbers as digits** (3 steps, 48 hours), except at the start of a sentence.

### 8. Language

Each text in one language. Don't mix English terms into Portuguese when a
Portuguese word exists and is used (`entrega`, not `delivery` of a project;
`painel`, not `dashboard`, unless the reader says dashboard).

## Part 2. What each project decides

### Who speaks

| Value | Who speaks | Fits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `we` | the team, as "we" (PT: "a gente", never "nós" or "nossa empresa") | studios, agencies, small companies |
| `I` | one person, signing the work | freelancers, portfolios, personal sites |
| `product` | nobody: the product describes itself | apps, tools, SaaS, docs |

The reader is always **"you"** (PT: **"você"**), in all three.

### Register

Same voice, two registers.

**Selling** (home, landing, proposal). Sit in the reader's chair. Sell the fit,
not the tool.

> You run the business. We handle the digital side.
> Not: *We offer complete digital solutions.*

**Product** (interface, README, docs, emails from the app). Verb first, the real
action, the reason when it helps. A table when it is a fact.

> Scans your area, finds businesses without a website, scores them and writes
> the first message. Everything runs locally.

## Part 3. Patterns

### Headlines

Say what the reader gets or does, in their words. A clever headline that needs
the subheading to make sense is two weak lines.

| Weak | Better |
| --- | --- |
| Welcome to Juniper Bakery | Bread out of the oven at 7. Cakes by order. |
| Innovative solutions for your business | Invoices sent, paid and filed, without a spreadsheet. |
| Your journey starts here | Open an account in 4 minutes. |

### Buttons and links

Verb + object, what happens when it is pressed. The button finishes the
sentence "I want to…".

| Weak | Better |
| --- | --- |
| Submit | Send the order |
| Click here | See the menu |
| Learn more | How pickup works |
| OK | Got it / Delete the file |
| Continue | Continue to payment |

A link's text makes sense alone, out of context (screen readers list links by text).

### Forms

- Label every field above it. A placeholder is an example, not a label.
- Say why you ask for anything personal: "Phone, so we can text you when it's ready."
- Mark the optional fields, not the required ones, when most are required.

### Errors

What happened, in plain words, and what to do now. Never blame the reader.

| Weak | Better |
| --- | --- |
| Invalid input | The date must be at least 2 days from today. |
| Error 500. Something went wrong. | We couldn't save the order. Your cart is safe, try again in a minute. |
| Field required | Add a phone number so we can confirm the order. |

### Empty states

Say what will appear here and how to make it appear.

> No orders yet. They show up here as soon as a customer pays.

### Confirmations and destructive actions

Name the thing and the consequence. The button repeats the action.

> Delete "Spring menu"? The 14 items in it are deleted too.
> [Keep it] [Delete the menu]

### Numbers, dates and prices

Real and specific: "48 hours", "$9", "from 7 to 3". Ranges when it varies,
never "affordable" or "fast".

## Part 4. Language notes

### English

- Sentence case for headings and buttons ("See today's menu"), not Title Case.
- Contractions are fine and sound human: you'll, we're, don't.
- Active voice: "We confirm on WhatsApp", not "Orders are confirmed via WhatsApp".

### Português do Brasil

- Nada de Title Case em títulos: "Veja o cardápio de hoje", não "Veja o Cardápio de Hoje".
- `Pra` no lugar de `para` soa natural em texto leve; em contrato e documento formal, `para`.
- Fora o gerundismo: "vamos enviar", não "vamos estar enviando".
- Fora "a nível de", "no sentido de", "venho por meio desta".
- "Você" sempre; nunca "o usuário" falando com o próprio usuário.
- Crase e concordância contam: um erro de português derruba a confiança do texto inteiro.

---

## Part 5. The check before publishing

Any "yes" means rewrite.

1. Would a dentist, a lawyer and a factory owner understand it right away?
2. Is there a word only people in the field use?
3. Does it assume what the reader wants?
4. Does it talk about the company instead of what the reader gets?
5. Is there an em dash, decorative capitals, emoji or an exclamation mark?
6. Is there a number, a case or a proof that does not exist?
